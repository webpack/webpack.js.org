At the end of October, I flew from the US to Munich, Germany, to represent webpack at the Google Summer of Code (GSoC) Mentor Summit 2026, along with over 180 other mentoring organizations. It was my first time at the Mentor Summit, and I could not have been more proud to represent webpack and the broader open source community.


## The project

Together with Claudio Wunder and Sebastian Beltran, I mentored webpack's "New Documentation Website" project. Our contributors built [webpack-doc-kit](https://github.com/webpack/webpack-doc-kit), which takes webpack's TypeScript declarations, runs them through TypeDoc, and hands the result to Node.js's [doc-kit](https://github.com/nodejs/doc-kit) for linking and UI. Once this tooling replaces webpack's legacy website, a release will produce updated docs on its own. You can try it today at [webpack-doc-kit.vercel.app](https://webpack-doc-kit.vercel.app), and we're planning to ship it alongside webpack 6.

We had the amazing opportunity to split up this work between three GSoC students:

- **Mohamed Shams El-Deen** worked on type parsing and content.

- **Tushar Thakur** worked on routing, navigation and UI.

- **Nikhil Kumar Rajak** worked on the release pipeline, CI and deployment.

## The summit

The Mentor Summit is an "unconference", so instead of a schedule fixed in advance, attendees propose and attend the sessions once they arrive at the conference. That means the agenda reflects what mentors actually want to talk about at the summit itself. This year, that theme was **culture**.

---

GSoC mentoring means working with people from all over the world, and from many different cultures:

- In the real world and open-source, **high-context and low-context cultures** communicate very  differently. In low-context cultures, you say exactly what you mean. In high-context cultures, much of the meaning is inferred by what you don't say.

- To be a good mentor, you need to understand both. You need to be explicit about expectations and vocally welcome pushback from contributors. At the same time, a contributor who only says "okay" may not actually be agreeing with you, so a one-on-one check-in can often go a long away to reveal information that would otherwise be left unsaid.
