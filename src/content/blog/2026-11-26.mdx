At the end of October, I flew from the US to Munich, Germany, to represent webpack at the Google Summer of Code (GSoC) Mentor Summit 2026, along with over 180 other mentoring organizations. It was my first time at the Mentor Summit, and I could not have been more proud to represent webpack, and FOSS in general.

Together with Claudio Wunder and Sebastian Beltran, I mentored webpack's "New Documentation Website" project. Our contributors built [webpack-doc-kit](https://github.com/webpack/webpack-doc-kit), which takes webpack's TypeScript declarations, runs them through TypeDoc, and hands the result to Node.js's [doc-kit](https://github.com/nodejs/doc-kit) for linking and UI. Once this tooling replaces webpack's legacy website, a release will produce updated docs on its own.

We had the amazing opportunity to split up this work between three GSoC students:

- **Mohamed Shams El-Deen** took type parsing and content.
- **Tushar Thakur** took routing, navigation and UI.
- **Nikhil Kumar Rajak** took the release pipeline, CI and deployment.

A large focus of this year's "unconference" was the differences between cultures

- **High-context and low-context cultures communicate differently.** In low-context cultures, you say exactly what you mean. In high-context cultures, much of the meaning lives in the relationship and what goes unsaid.&#32;
- **Good mentors bridge both ways.** Be explicit about expectations and say out loud that pushback is welcome. At the same time, a quiet "okay" isn't always agreement, and a one-on-one check-in will often reveal information that would otherwise be left unsaid.
